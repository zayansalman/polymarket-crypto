shares a trade

A share pays $1 if it wins, so 5 shares at 70¢ cost $3.50. Polymarket's smallest order is 5 shares. Bigger trades don't improve the odds, they only make the wins and losses bigger.

Trade size is currently a dollar range, which the bot's $3-per-trade safety limit blocks. Saving switches it to shares.

trades

1 means one trade at a time. With more, the bot can buy again in a window it already holds and start the next window before the last one settles. Trades in the same window win or lose together, so extra trades make the swings bigger, like a bigger trade size.

seconds

After a trade, the bot waits at least this long before buying again in the same window, so it doesn't buy one price several times in a row.

¢ a share

How much the bot's estimate must beat the asking price before it buys. Higher means fewer, pickier trades.

Every hour the bot checks how its past chances turned out and corrects them, partly trusting the market's own prices. It uses the correction only while, replayed with your settings over past windows, the corrected chances make more money after fees than its raw math.

The bot subtracts the trading fee (about 1.75¢ a share at 50¢, less near 0¢ or 100¢) before comparing its edge with the minimum. The practice balance pays the fee either way.
